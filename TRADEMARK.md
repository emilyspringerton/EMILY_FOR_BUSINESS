# Emily for Business — Trademark Policy

Version 1.0 — 2026-09-03

**Modeled directly on the real, checked Chef Software trademark model** (Chef's own source code
is Apache 2.0 — fully open — but a separate trademark policy restricts who may call a
distribution "Chef": anyone may redistribute the code, but must strip Chef's own trademarks from
it, so only Chef's own official builds carry the Chef name). This document does the same for
"Emily for Business," separately from `LICENSE.md`, which governs the code itself.

**Not reviewed by a lawyer — see `LICENSE.md`'s own "Before this is real" section, which applies
equally here.**

## What is covered

- The name **"Emily for Business"** (and any confusingly similar variant).
- Any logo, wordmark, or visual branding associated with the product.
- Any domain name, social handle, or package/registry name that uses the name or a confusingly
  similar variant, when used to refer to a distribution of this software or a derivative of it.

## What you may always do

- Refer to "Emily for Business" by name in truthful, non-branding statements — e.g. "this tool
  is compatible with Emily for Business," "based on Emily for Business," "a fork of Emily for
  Business" — the same nominative-use latitude the real Chef policy and most real trademark
  policies (Docker, WordPress, Kubernetes) grant.
- Redistribute the source code itself under the terms of `LICENSE.md`.

## What requires the name/branding to be removed

If you redistribute the software, or a modified version of it, to anyone outside your own
organization (as `LICENSE.md` Section 2 already restricts without a separate agreement, but
this applies even where a separate agreement permits redistribution unless that agreement says
otherwise):

1. You must **remove or replace** the "Emily for Business" name, wordmark, and logo from the
   distribution's own product name, package name, and primary branding.
2. You may **state truthfully** that your distribution is derived from or based on Emily for
   Business (nominative use, see above) — you just cannot present your own distribution AS
   Emily for Business, or as an official Emily for Business release.
3. Only builds published directly by the Licensor (see `LICENSE.md` §6 for who that is) may be
   called "Emily for Business" without qualification.

## Why this split exists

Real, direct rationale, matching the founder's own explicit ask: the code license (`LICENSE.md`)
controls what people may DO with the software; this document controls who may CALL something
"Emily for Business." Chef Software's own real-world reasoning for keeping these separate,
preserved because it's the actual, correct reasoning, not reinvented here: it avoids confusion
about which distributions are official (supported, warrantied, indemnified by the Licensor) and
which are unofficial third-party forks — a security- and support-relevant distinction, not just
branding pedantry.

## Contact

should go to the Licensor directly — no dedicated contact channel exists yet; this is a real,
named gap for whoever operates this repository to fill in once Emily for Business has a real
public-facing presence.
